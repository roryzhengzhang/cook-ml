Cook ML is a place to learn machine learning the way a recipe teaches cooking: one step at a time, with every ingredient measured out. Each tutorial starts from a familiar example, builds the mechanism, writes the math, and then works it through on real numbers you can check by hand.

Hi, I'm Zheng. I'm an applied scientist and machine learning engineer on the AI team at Adobe Experience Platform. These days I work on how the Adobe CX Enterprise Coworker, an AI agent for enterprise customer experience work, reasons through a task and how people work alongside it.

Most of what I build sits between human-computer interaction and machine learning. I'm drawn to AI tools that help while you're in the middle of something hard, like planning an argument, brainstorming with a team in one room, or reading a story with your kid, and that still leave the thinking to you. That work has appeared at CHI, UIST and IUI.

Before Adobe, I did my PhD in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame, working with Toby Li. Before that I earned two master's degrees in computer science, at the University of Rochester with Zhen Bai and at the University of Minnesota with Haiyi Zhu, and I started out studying software engineering at Shaanxi Normal University. Along the way I interned as a research scientist at Apple, AWS AI and Meta Reality Labs.

## Why I write Cook ML

Honestly, I started Cook ML for myself.

ML moves fast. Every week brings a new paper, a new model, or a new trick some lab swears by, and my day job keeps me close to language-model agents. But I noticed a gap. I could follow the headlines, yet when I sat down to reason about a real system, the basics sometimes got fuzzy. Why is the loss a log? Where does a 7B model's memory actually go? What does a key-value cache store? I'd look it up, find either a hand-wavy analogy or a wall of notation, and move on without really getting it.

So I wanted one place that does three things for me:

- **Review the essentials.** The core ideas I lean on every day, written clearly enough that I can come back and refresh them in ten minutes.
- **Go deep when I need to.** Not just what a method does, but why it works, with the math and a worked example I can check by hand.
- **Keep up with what's new.** Recent research and what teams are actually shipping in industry, tied back to the fundamentals so new ideas have somewhere to land.

Cook ML is that place. I write it in public in case it helps you too, especially if you're fairly new to the field but want more than the surface.

Every chapter follows the same recipe: an everyday example first, then the mechanism, then the equation, then a calculation with real numbers.
