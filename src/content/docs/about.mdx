Cook ML teaches machine learning the way a good recipe teaches cooking. Every ingredient is measured out, every step comes in order, and nothing is left to guesswork. The tutorials run from the fundamentals of large language models to the techniques behind today's frontier systems, and every idea is worked through on real numbers that you can check by hand.

Hi, I'm Zheng. I'm an applied scientist and machine learning engineer on the AI team at Adobe Experience Platform, where I build the Adobe CX Enterprise Coworker, an AI agent for enterprise customer experience work. My work spans the agent's planning and multi-agent orchestration, the benchmark and regression pipelines that gate each release, and diagnosing its behavior from production traces across large enterprise deployments.

Before Adobe, I worked on machine learning in industry research labs as a research scientist intern at Apple, AWS AI and Meta Reality Labs.

My research background is in human–AI interaction, and it still shapes how I build models and agents today. I care about AI that makes people more capable while leaving the judgment with them. I did my PhD in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame with Toby Li, after master's degrees in computer science at the University of Rochester and the University of Minnesota, and I started out studying software engineering at Shaanxi Normal University.

## Why I write Cook ML

Honestly, I started Cook ML for myself. In graduate school I took machine learning courses from the basics all the way to advanced topics, and at the time it all made sense. Years later, much of it had quietly slipped away. Knowledge you don't use every week fades faster than you'd think. Call it brain rot.

Meanwhile, the field refuses to slow down. A new model, paper or training trick lands almost every week, and each one builds on a stack of earlier ideas that its authors assume you already know. My day job keeps me close to language-model agents, so I could always follow the headlines. Knowing how a new method actually works, and what it really costs, is another matter. The details live in long technical reports that assume you've read the last dozen papers, and the explanations around them tend to be either a one-line summary or a wall of notation. There's rarely time to work through each one properly, so the list of ideas I wanted to truly understand kept growing.

So I wanted one place that does three things for me:

- **Review the essentials.** The core ideas I lean on every day, written clearly enough that I can come back and refresh them in ten minutes.
- **Go deep when I need to.** Not just what a method does, but why it works, with the math and a worked example I can check by hand.
- **Keep up with what's new.** Recent research and what teams are actually shipping in industry, tied back to the fundamentals so new ideas have somewhere to land.

Cook ML is that place. I write it in public in case it helps you too, especially if you're fairly new to the field but want more than the surface.

You might ask why anyone needs a site like this when an AI assistant can answer almost any ML question in seconds. I use those assistants every day, and they're great at answering the question you already know to ask. What's harder to get from them, one answer at a time, is the structure underneath: how the ideas connect, which parts are essential, and where a new method fits among the old ones. That structure helps you connect the dots between answers, and it points you to follow-up questions an AI might not suggest but that are well worth asking if you want to learn. It only forms when you work through the ideas yourself, because understanding is the one part of learning you can't outsource. Cook ML is meant to be that structure, an anchor you can come back to and hang every new answer on.
